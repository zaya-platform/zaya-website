# Management brief

## Why this matters

Every day MSI Ethiopia's centres and maternity centres serve clients whose experience decides whether they return, recommend us, and trust us with sensitive care. Today most of what those clients think reaches management late, on paper, in one language, and without a reliable path to action. Serious concerns — a safeguarding worry, a client coerced or turned away, an illegal fee — can sit in a suggestion box for weeks.

The proposed **Client Feedback System (CFS)** gives every client a private, easy way to be heard in their own language, and gives management the evidence and the alerts to act the same day.

## What management gets

- **A live picture of client experience** by centre, region, service line, language and channel, on any device, updated within a minute of feedback arriving.
- **Immediate flags for critical issues.** Safeguarding, abuse, coercion or denial of a legal service, clinical harm, confidentiality breaches and illegal fees trigger an e-mail and SMS to the responsible managers within minutes, open a tracked case, and escalate automatically if nobody acknowledges.
- **Reports written for decisions**: daily site digests, weekly regional summaries, a monthly national client-voice report and a quarterly board pack, with AI-drafted narrative that a manager approves before it goes out. All figures come from the data; none are generated.
- **An "ask the data" assistant** that answers questions such as "which centres had the most privacy complaints last quarter" with the numbers, a chart and the query behind them.
- **A closed loop with clients**: TV screens in waiting areas show aggregated satisfaction and "You said → We did" actions, so clients see that feedback changes things.
- **Evidence for donors and MSI Global**: a documented complaint-and-feedback mechanism with response times, aligned to MSI client-voice indicators and feeding MSIE's existing Power BI reports.

## What clients get

- Feedback in **Amharic, Afaan Oromoo, Tigrinya, Somali, Sidaamu Afoo, Afar, Wolaytta or English**, by short form, free text or **voice**.
- A kiosk at the exit, a QR code for their own phone, SMS, Telegram or the hotline — whichever they prefer, and all of it works when the network is down.
- **Anonymity by default**, explicit consent for anything else, and no personal detail ever shown on a screen.
- Prompts read aloud for clients who prefer not to read, large buttons, and non-judgemental wording reviewed by MSIE's clinical and client-experience teams.

## How it is built and run

- A modern web application that installs like an app on phones and tablets, with a manager portal for laptops and a display mode for TVs.
- **Hosted on MSI Ethiopia's own server and domain**, with encrypted backups off-site; a cloud option exists if MSIE prefers.
- An AI layer that transcribes, translates, classifies and summarises, with personal identifiers removed before any AI processing, every AI output reviewable by staff, and an option to run all AI processing inside Ethiopia if data-protection policy requires.
- **Multi-country by design**: another MSI programme is a new configuration, not a new system.
- Compliant with Ethiopia's Personal Data Protection Proclamation (1321/2024) and MSI's safeguarding and data-protection policies; a Data Protection Impact Assessment is completed before the pilot.

## Roadmap

| Phase | Indicative duration | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0 · Discovery and design | 4–6 weeks | Validate assumptions with MSIE teams, site visits, forms and escalation workshops, DPIA, hosting decision, prototypes tested with clients |
| 1 · Pilot | 10–12 weeks build + 12 weeks pilot | Two core centres and one maternity centre; kiosk, QR, voice, critical alerts, dashboards, display screens |
| 2 · National roll-out | 3–6 months | All centres and maternity centres, all languages, SMS/Telegram/hotline channels, full reporting, Power BI integration |
| 3 · Multi-country | ongoing | Onboarding kit for other MSI programmes, advanced assistants, MSI Global aggregation |

## Decisions requested from management

1. Approve the discovery phase and nominate a product owner (Client Experience or MEL) and an IT lead.
2. Confirm the pilot sites (two core centres, one maternity centre) and the pilot languages.
3. Decide the hosting option: MSIE server, an Ethiopian data centre, or MSI Global cloud.
4. Decide the AI data position: cloud AI on redacted text under a data-processing agreement, or in-country processing only.
5. Confirm the escalation owners and the safeguarding reporting route the system must follow.
6. Approve budget lines for hardware (tablets, screens), SMS, AI usage, translation and training; figures will be sized in discovery.

## What is deliberately not in scope

The system does not keep clinical records, book appointments, appraise individual staff, or replace MSI's formal incident and safeguarding case systems. It routes to them and records the hand-off.

## Assumptions to validate

Statements about MSIE's channels, systems and current feedback practice in the Business Requirements Document are working assumptions drawn from public material. They are listed for confirmation in discovery and should not be quoted externally.
